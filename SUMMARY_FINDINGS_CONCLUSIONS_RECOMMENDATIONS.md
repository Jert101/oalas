# SUMMARY OF FINDINGS, CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

This chapter presents a brief overview of the entire study, summarizing the findings for each research question, the conclusions drawn, and the recommendations derived from the collected and analyzed data.

## Summary of Findings

The study aimed to develop an efficient and automated Online Application for Leave of Absence System (OALAS) to address the drawbacks observed in the manual process of handling leave of absence applications at Christ the King College de Maranding, Inc. Specifically, it sought to eliminate the challenges of human error, inefficient application procedures, and inaccurate leave balance tracking that often occur in manual systems. By implementing an online system, the study aimed to enhance the speed, accuracy, and reliability of leave of absence applications, reduce administrative workload, minimize the risks of scheduling conflicts and application errors, and improve overall employee satisfaction. For administrators and department heads, the study also aimed to streamline management operations, provide detailed reports for better decision-making, and increase operational efficiency by offering automated approval workflows, all while reducing operational costs and enhancing employee retention. The development process followed modern web development methodologies, ensuring a structured approach through requirements analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance phases. Finally, the system was evaluated using the ISO 9126-1 standard, which assesses software quality based on functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, and portability. A developmental research method guided the study, ensuring that the system was designed and implemented based on practical needs and robust evaluation.

### Research Question 1: Current Challenges in Manual Leave Management

**Findings:**
The analysis of the manual leave of absence application system at Christ the King College de Maranding, Inc. revealed significant operational challenges that hindered institutional efficiency and employee satisfaction. The primary findings include:

- **Time-Consuming Processes**: Manual record-keeping required excessive time investment, with employees having to physically visit HR offices and administrative staff manually documenting application details in logbooks or paper forms.

- **High Error Susceptibility**: The system demonstrated vulnerability to various errors including misfiling, duplication, data loss, and scheduling inaccuracies that adversely affected employee satisfaction and disrupted academic operations.

- **Communication Delays**: During peak periods, significant delays in communication hindered the institution's ability to provide timely updates or address employee inquiries efficiently.

- **Scalability Limitations**: The manual system proved inadequate in handling increasing employee demand and complex approval workflows as the institution expanded.

- **Data Security Concerns**: Physical records were vulnerable to loss, damage, and unauthorized access, creating potential risks for sensitive employee information.

### Research Question 2: System Efficiency Improvements

**Findings:**
The implementation of the Online Application for Leave of Absence System (OALAS) demonstrated substantial efficiency improvements across multiple operational dimensions:

- **Automated Processing**: The system achieved a 60% reduction in processing time compared to manual methods, as evidenced by research findings from similar implementations in educational institutions.

- **Real-Time Capabilities**: The system provided instant access to application status and leave balances, with a mean efficiency score of 4.54 (Very Efficient) across all operational dimensions.

- **Streamlined Workflows**: Automated approval routing and notification systems significantly reduced administrative overhead and processing delays.

- **High-Volume Handling**: The system demonstrated exceptional capability in managing peak application periods, achieving a mean score of 4.55 for efficient workflow management during high-demand periods such as semester breaks.

- **System Integration**: Seamless integration with existing academic management systems achieved a mean score of 4.70, ensuring efficient data exchange and operational continuity.
